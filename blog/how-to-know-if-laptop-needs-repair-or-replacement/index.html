How to Know If Your Laptop Needs Repair or Replacement

A practical Johannesburg guide for deciding when a slow or damaged laptop should be repaired, upgraded or replaced.

Start with the value of the laptop

A laptop is worth repairing when the fault is isolated, the body is still in good condition, and the repair cost is reasonable compared with replacing the full machine. A screen, keyboard, battery, SSD or RAM upgrade can often extend the life of a business laptop for much less than buying new.

When repair makes sense

Repair usually makes sense when the laptop is business-grade, still fast enough for your work, and only has one clear fault. Examples include a cracked screen on an HP ProBook, a failing battery on a Lenovo ThinkPad, a broken hinge on a Dell Latitude, or a slow machine that still has a working motherboard.

When replacement may be better

Replacement may be better when the motherboard is damaged, the body is broken in multiple places, the screen and battery are both expensive, or the device is too old to run current business software reliably.

Ask for assessment before deciding

Do not guess from symptoms only. Send the model number, photos and the problem description. A short assessment can prevent spending money on the wrong part.
